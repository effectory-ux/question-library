Do we test A vs B — and if not, what do we test?

A short brief on where the A/B discussion landed, why a preference test between the two won't produce a decision, and what a session can still answer — including method and participants.

The two models, as they stand now

A — Fully customizable library

One merged catalog: custom and Effectory questions live together. The coordinator configures order, naming and grouping. Publishing pushes the whole curated view as the search bucket used in survey/template creation.

B — Standard / custom split

Effectory's catalog is immutable (only controlled variant texts for benchmarked questions). Custom content lives in a separate, company-owned collection where the coordinator manages availability. Publish pushes only new custom content and variant choices.

Shared in both — and this is the crux: custom questions, template creation and editing, benchmark protection (variant or copy-to-custom, never free editing), publish as a failsafe, and full flexibility at survey and template level. Nothing ever syncs into existing surveys or templates in either model.

Is a comparative A/B test the way forward?

Preference A/B: not meaningful Assumption testing: yes A-vs-B call: team decision

Every task a participant can perform succeeds in both versions — creation, templates, variants, publishing all exist in both. Task success will be identical, so a session would measure ordering effects and surface polish, then hand us a preference we'd launder as evidence. The differences that remain are real but second-order: they only show up at organizational scale and over time (curation burden, junk-drawer growth, publish legibility), which a first-session click-through structurally cannot surface.

The remaining trade-offs are strategic, not behavioral:

  • Reversibility — shipping B and unlocking curation later is an upgrade; shipping A and locking it down later takes a capability away from customers. One-way vs two-way door.
  • Positioning — B's structure visibly protects the validated, benchmarked core (Effectory's differentiator); A blurs whose content is whose.
  • Tooling cost — A only works with placement flows, publish diffs and partial publish; B needs almost none of that.
  • The one condition where A wins — enterprise accounts with large custom-content volume and browse-heavy coordinators. That's answerable from usage analytics and CS input this week, not from six lab sessions.

Recommendation: the team decides (proposed default: B, on reversibility + cost + positioning), with one named falsifier checked in analytics: how much custom content do our largest accounts actually hold today? Research budget goes to validating the chosen model's risky assumptions instead.

What we can test — four assumptions, one prototype

These are evaluative, not comparative. Each has a real wrong answer that would cost us in production.

AssumptionProbeSignal we need
Copy-on-use"You just fixed this custom question in the library. What happened to the running survey that uses it?"Do users share our no-sync model, or expect propagation? Wrong expectations = silent data damage.
Variant vs copyAsk them to reword a benchmarked question; stop before they act: "what does this do to your benchmark?"Variant = safe, copy = custom without benchmark. Must be understood before acting.
Publish legibilityAt the publish confirm: "in your own words — what changes tomorrow for a manager creating a survey?"Accurate, confident answers. If people publish blind, the failsafe is theater.
Approval / gatekeepingSeeded queue of manager-submitted questions incl. a near-duplicate of a standard one. "Make Lisa's question available — and only that."Will coordinators accept the curator role, and can they handle duplicates?

Add one seeded-scale task: a custom collection of ±30 realistic questions accumulated over a year (gems, near-duplicates, stale ones). "Find the expense-tool question from last spring, then make this presentable for the annual wave." This makes the long-term cost of the chosen model experienceable instead of hypothetical.

If the team still wants a head-to-head

Then only these four moments discriminate — build the script on them and cut everything both versions share (creation, templates, variants):

  1. Placement — approving a manager's question: curation work (A) vs availability toggle (B). Does placement read as meaningful or as homework?
  2. The downstream payoff — both prototypes must show the survey-creation picker after publishing, or A's entire benefit stays invisible and B wins by default.
  3. Findability at scale — the seeded 30-question collection, in A's curated topics vs B's flat bucket.
  4. The mixed batch — pending state in A holds one legitimate change and one accidental reorder; publish only the legitimate one. In B this dilemma can't exist.

Counterbalance version order (the current plan fixes B first), and agree the decision rule before sessions — e.g. "B wins if placement reads as homework and the mixed batch fails; A wins if the curated picker visibly lands and the flat bucket frustrates search." If results point both ways, the landing zone is the hybrid: immutable core, custom content slotted into that structure.

Who to recruit

  • Central coordinators, 5–6 — the primary audience for everything above; the library is their back office. Prioritize accounts that already use custom questions, and include at least two large accounts (many collaborators / heavy custom content) since that's where the models actually diverge.
  • Managers / delegated creators, 3–4 — only two things concern them: submitting a question for approval, and the survey-creation picker (one curated list vs two shelves). Short sessions; they should never see the library administration itself.
  • Internal CS/CSM input in parallel: custom-content volume per account and existing "can I change this question" tickets — this answers the enterprise falsifier without any sessions.

Material: both prototype versions exist as clickable DS-built prototypes with a version switcher (toolbar) and can be seeded with the messy states above. The publish-diff review in A is not yet designed — that must be settled before any A/B session, or the comparison is rigged against A.
